## The problem

I wanted a clear picture of my money. Monthly bills, the bills that come only once or twice a year and everyday spending all ran together, and at the end of the month there was usually nothing left to save.

Bank apps didn’t help much. They show what you spent in each category, but they count every card payment as yours. If I pay for something and someone pays me back half, the bank still says I spent the whole amount. So the numbers never told the truth.

## What I built

Säästöt follows a proper budgeting method: plan the month first, set money aside for bills and for the bills that come rarely, and save towards goals that have a date. The app does the maths. My only job is to sort each purchase when it comes in.

- **Purchases come in automatically.** Card payments show up from the bank, so I can’t forget any. I pick a category and say how much of it was mine: all, half, a custom amount or none of it.
- **Today** shows how much is left this month, how much I can spend per day, and how much I’ll save if I stay on budget.
- **Plan** is where I set up the month. I see straight away how much I’ll have saved by the end of it.
- **Saving goals** tell me if I’ll reach them on time, and what to change if I won’t.
- **History** shows how much I’ve saved over time.

## The hard parts

### Keeping financial data safe

The app holds real bank data, so security was the first design question, not the last. Each user’s data is encrypted with their own key before it is stored, so a copy of the database on its own is useless. On top of that, every table has row-level security, each phone has to be registered, and the app opens with a PIN or a fingerprint. I keep the details private on purpose, which is also why the code isn’t public.

### Encrypted, but still useful

Encryption makes the server’s job harder. It can’t read the data, but it still has to sync the bank, drop duplicate payments and suggest which bill a payment belongs to. Getting those to work together was the biggest design problem in the project.

### Purchases that change after the fact

A card purchase first shows up as a pending reservation. Later it turns into the real charge, sometimes with a different amount, and sometimes after the month has already been closed. The app matches the two and fixes the difference without changing a closed month. Tests check that moving a payment around never changes a month’s totals.

## Where it is now

I use it every day and keep improving it. There’s a [public demo](https://demo.saastot.buddyfall.com/) with made-up data, so anyone can try it. Only the demo is public. The real app and its code stay private.
